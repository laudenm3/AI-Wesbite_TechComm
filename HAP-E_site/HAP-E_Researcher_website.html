LLM Research Dashboard

A comparative guide to the stylistic, rhetorical, and emotional differences in text generated by large language models compared to human authors

This website uses data from HAP-E-2: the Human-AI Parallel English corpus of texts, which can be accessed here: https://huggingface.co/datasets/browndw/human-ai-parallel-corpus-2.
The stylistic analysis was performed using the python package PyBiber: https://browndw.github.io/pybiber/. The sentiment analysis was conducted with Google's GoEmotions dataset: https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/master/goemotions.
Ultimately, this website draws on research encapsulated by the following articles: Markey, B., Brown, D. W., Laudenbach, M., & Kohler, A. (2024). Dense and disconnected: Analyzing the sedimented style of ChatGPT-generated text at scale. Written Communication, 41(4), 571–600; and Reinhart, A., Markey, B., Laudenbach, M., Pantusen, K., Yurko, R., Weinberg, G., & Brown, D. W. (2025). Do LLMs write like humans? Variation in grammatical and rhetorical styles. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 122(8), e2422455122.

Two independent highlighting systems. Biber dimensions (backgrounds) group the 67 Biber features by the factor loadings used in Markey et al. (2024) — Involved vs. Informational (Dim 1) and Overt Argumentation (Dim 4). Thematic groups (underlines) bundle features by the contrasts most cited in Reinhart et al. (2025) — nominal density, hedging & stance, clausal coordination, and agentless/impersonal constructions. The Show highlights in toggle governs both systems. By default they are sign-split: Sample A only lights up features that load positively on the active MDA factor; Sample B only lights up features with negative loadings. Switch to Both samples to light up every feature in an active group on both sides regardless of sign. Because many features participate in both systems, a single token can show both a background and an underline at once.
Every sentence in the corpus was scored for 27 emotions with a GoEmotions classifier.
